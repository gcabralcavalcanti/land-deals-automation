# How it works

## How I use it

This model is new: I built it for this portfolio, and it does not replace a routine I run today. It puts into code the
questions I worked through on each land deal: what the plot really costs to buy and hold, whether splitting it into
lots pays more than selling it whole, how much cash goes in before sales start, and when the money comes back. The way
I would use it is one deal at a time: describe the plot in a text file, check the inputs the AI pulls out, run the
model, and read the numbers and the note before deciding. Nothing runs on a schedule, and the inputs only enter the
model after I accept them.

## Why this model

Land development ties up money for years: you buy the land, pay for approvals, streets, water and power, and only
then start selling, often in monthly installments. A deal that looks profitable on paper can still be a bad use of
money if the cash comes back too slowly. This model puts every cost and every payment on a monthly calendar, so that
question has a number.

## The steps

1. **Split or sell whole.** Large urban plots are split into lots; small and rural ones are sold whole. The workbook
   shows both options for every urban plot, so the rule can be challenged.
2. **Costs, month by month.** Purchase taxes and registry, upkeep and property tax while the land is unsold, and for
   lots: surveys and approvals, streets and utilities, marketing, sales commission and taxes on sales.
3. **Sales and payments.** Lots sell over about three years. Buyers pay part in cash and part in monthly installments
   with interest, adjusted for inflation once a year; a small share is never paid.
4. **Results.** Value created, return per year, cash tied up, months to get the money back, with a bad, an expected
   and a good case, and a chart of which assumption changes the answer most.
5. **AI where it helps.** Claude reads a plot description written in plain Portuguese ("12 hectares, should close at
   R$ 3.8 million...") and fills in the model's inputs, which a person confirms. It also writes a short investment
   note per plot; the code rejects any note that quotes a number the model did not compute.
6. **Checked twice.** A separate calculation rebuilds three plots from scratch and must match the model.

Every assumption (tax rates, building costs, sales speed, interest) is in `land_deals/config.py`, with a range and
where it comes from: the law that sets it, or "illustrative" when it is a typical value.

## Results on the made-up portfolio

For 10 invented plots (full report: [`final-report.pdf`](../final-report.pdf)):

| | Expected case | Bad case | Good case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total sales | R$ 59.5 million | R$ 50.4 million | R$ 65.6 million |
| Value created, after a 14% a year required return | R$ 2.2 million | R$ -11.8 million | R$ 12.3 million |
| Return per year | 16.6% | 2.8% | 31.5% |
| Most cash tied up at one time | R$ 28.4 million | R$ 39.9 million | R$ 20.4 million |
| Months until the money is back | 61 | 99 | 44 |

Splitting the large plots into lots is what makes money. The six plots resold whole earn less than the 14% required
return: holding land costs upkeep and property tax every month, and the land gains value more slowly. The cost of
building streets and utilities moves the result more than anything else.

## What the demo writes

`python demo.py` writes `final-report.pdf` and, in `output/`: an Excel workbook (`land_portfolio.xlsx`), a dashboard
page (`dashboard.html`), the investment notes (`memos.md`) and the headline numbers (`summary.json`).

The AI answers used by the demo are saved in `data/recorded/`, so it runs without an API key and costs nothing. With a
key, only questions never asked before are sent, and each is saved. Every call is logged in `output/logs/`.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `python demo.py` | the full example (`--live` to call the API for questions never asked) |
| `python generate_data.py` | recreate the made-up portfolio in `data/portfolio.json` |
| `python -m land_deals run` | rebuild the model and all outputs (`--no-memos` for no AI at all) |
| `python -m land_deals intake <file.txt>` | read a plot description and show the inputs it found (`--accept` adds the plot) |

## Data

All data is synthetic, generated by `generate_data.py`: the plots, towns, prices and the three plot descriptions in
`data/intake/` are invented. No real company, client or deal is used.

## Other limits

- Inflation is a fixed yearly rate, not the published index.
- Cancelled sales and mixed plans (split part of a plot, sell the rest) are not modelled.
